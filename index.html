How day and night are split. A streak is the time between two interactions, on any device, and is never cut at a clock boundary unless it’s still going at 22:00 or 07:00. It counts as day or night according to the check that starts it: a streak that starts from 22:00 until 06:00 is night, and one that starts from 06:00 until 22:00 is day. So a screen-free 4–10pm is a day streak, sleep that begins with a 22:56 check is a night streak, a 3am check starts another night streak (breaking it, but staying night), and a check at 6:15am starts a day streak instead. But a streak with no interaction at all is force-cut at 22:00 (day→night) and again at 07:00 (night→day), as many times as it keeps going — so a quiet 8:18pm to 10am becomes a short evening day streak, a full night streak, and a fresh morning day streak, rather than one streak that swallows the whole stretch or gets credited to the wrong day. A streak that already ends at or before a cut, via a real interaction, is never touched by it. Night interruptions are separate bursts of activity between midnight and 06:00. Streaks under 15 minutes are ignored in the tables above.

Daily score. Two scores, each out of 100, so a good phone day shows even on a day of heavy work-computer use. Computers (laptop, worklaptop and any other non-phone device): 40 for streaks, 60 for screen time. Phone: 30 for streaks, 50 for screen time, 20 for unlocks. Each score only looks at its own devices. Streaks: that group’s three longest waking streaks that day are summed uncapped and compared against a 12-hour total, so an 8h streak plus a 2h and a 30min one scores the same as three tidy 3-hour streaks — a big one no longer gets wasted. A streak starting in the 06:00-07:00 grace window is credited from 07:00 for scoring, so checking your phone at 6:30 can never score better than staying quiet until the automatic cutover. Night streaks don’t count here, so sleep doesn’t inflate the score. Screen: that group’s active spans are merged first (two computers in use in the same minute cost once, not twice), then 1 point is lost for every 10 minutes active that day, reaching zero at 500 minutes (8h20m) — there is no grace period, so any active time at all costs something. The screen-free percentage shown in the table is a separate figure across all devices and doesn’t translate directly into either score. Unlocks: full marks at 20 or fewer, zero at 50. A phone call never breaks a streak, never counts as active time and never counts as an unlock unless it’s outgoing. A score is only given once every device in its group that existed that day was logging all day (and, for the phone, was sending event types). A computer added later is ignored on the day it first logged — that day is scored on the other devices alone — and counts from the next day.

“Combined” only counts a gap when every device was quiet, from when the last of the original devices began logging; a device added later never affects earlier days, and is ignored on the day it first logs. Per-device figures are measured within the same day or night period; * = that device started logging partway through it. Monthly averages are worked out per day first — that day’s longest, second-longest and third-longest streak — and then averaged across the month, leaving out today and any day not fully and validly logged.
