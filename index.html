How day and night are split. A streak is the time between two interactions, on any device, and is never cut at a clock boundary. It counts as day or night according to the check that starts it: a streak that starts from 21:00 until 05:00 is night, and one that starts from 05:00 until 21:00 is day. So a screen-free 4–10pm is a day streak, sleep that begins with a 22:56 check is a night streak, a wake-up check at 3am starts another night streak, and a check at 5:30am starts a day streak. Night interruptions are separate bursts of activity between midnight and 05:00. Streaks under 15 minutes are ignored in the tables above.

Daily score. Out of 100. Up to 50 points for your three longest waking streaks (each earns full marks at 3 hours; streaks starting from 21:00 to 05:00 don’t count, so sleep doesn’t inflate the score), up to 30 for total screen time (full marks at 1 hour or less, zero at 8 hours), and up to 20 for phone unlocks (full marks at 20 or fewer, zero at 100). Screen time is computer active time plus phone time from unlock to lock. A day is only scored once every device was logging all day and the phone is sending event types (unlock and lock). Phone time between an unlock and the next lock counts as activity, not as a screen-free streak.

Daily activity. For computers, active time is the number of pings times 10 seconds; a ping means there was keyboard or mouse input in that 10-second slot, so time spent reading or watching without touching anything isn’t counted. For phones, pickups are estimated as half the unlock and lock pings. Days are midnight to midnight; * = logging started partway through that day.

“Combined” only counts a gap when every device was quiet, from when the last device began logging. Per-device figures are measured within the same day or night period; * = that device started logging partway through it. Monthly averages leave out the streak or night still in progress.
